# EME sales and marketing playbook

Source: transcript of `webapp/site/emeworld/proposals/browser_recording_20261111_110943-transcript.txt` (clips with timestamps below). The lessons are from the clips. The "For EME" lines are suggestions, not facts from the recording. Follow `eme-brand-voice` for all wording (no em dashes, short, bold key words, EME Profile not persona).

## 1. Introduce with value, not a title (0:00)
- Step 1, start with value: a promise, not a job title. "I help teams hit impossible deadlines without burning out" beats "senior project manager".
- Step 2, add a tiny hook that makes people curious ("most of my work lives behind the scenes"). Curiosity leads to follow-ups, follow-ups build relationships.
- Step 3, end with presence, not pressure ("if a project is ever stuck, I'm the one who pulls it back out").
- Goal: not to impress, to be remembered.
- For EME: "I help your community keep its knowledge and run its work with its own AI." Hook: the AI belongs to you, not a vendor.

## 2. Pick yourself (0:58)
- Do not wait to be invited, hired or published. Make the thing, send it to about 20 people, ask them to share it free. If it spreads, the phone rings. If not, make something better.
- For EME: ship a small, free, real example (a chat app, a skill, a demo server) to 20 target communities and let the good ones spread. Examples already on the site: UN News, Cacao Source, Cumbre de Cocreación, Courageous Dreamers.

## 3. Build a backend, do not live on the frontend (1:51)
- Cheap entry products ($29 to $197 courses, $39/month or $390/year membership) are a loss leader. Revenue per sale was $1.36 on average. Like a grocery store selling diapers at a loss without selling anything else.
- Fix: every buyer gets an onboarding call with a team member. Between purchase and call they watch a short video that pre-frames the call. For more: add a 7 day gap and homework between call 1 and call 2.
- That is where the $3,000 to $8,000 offer is sold.
- Use bridge language that connects product one to product two.
- For EME: free or low-cost server/skill entry, then onboarding call, then a paid setup, hosting or custom skills package. Keep the open source stack as the trust builder.

## 4. Never sell the pen (3:25)
- Ask what is going on in the prospect's life. Only if the product matches the need, present it as the solution.
- For EME: discovery first. What data, what team, what repeated work. Then show the one EME feature that solves it.

## 5. Quick rules (4:07)
Promise late, deliver early. Learn fast, trust slow. Bet big, test small. Give first, ask last. Ignore opinions. Always promote, never slander. Disregard doubters, persist. One speaker also says "I've been undercharging for years": check price before assuming demand is the problem.

## 6. Find the price with four questions (4:33)
Ask customers (Van Westendorp):
1. At what price is it so expensive you would not consider it?
2. At what price is it so cheap it could not be any good?
3. At what price is it at the edge, you would have to think but would buy?
4. At what price is it a good deal?
- Plot the four points. Marginal cheapness = too cheap to be believable. Marginal expensiveness = too expensive to consider. Use the area under the curve to find the price that earns the most.
- The speaker runs the data through an AI tool and gets the analysis in about six minutes.
- For EME: run this survey for each package (hosting, onboarding, custom skills) and let an EME agent do the analysis.

## 7. Video marketing now that AI makes it easy (6:53)
- Claim: video is the most effective digital marketing, but 99% of businesses have nobody who likes being on camera. The speaker says Claude Opus 5.5 made good video as easy as a good blog post.
- Mistake 1: aiming to go viral. A broad audience is not your buyer, so it converts poorly.
- Mistake 2: promotional content. Make exactly one promo video for your site and brand-name searches. Everything else should be explainer videos.
- Explainers: target the terms people search in Google or ChatGPT before buying, and start the video title with the search term. Google likes video in results and there is less competition than blogs.
- The transcript ends partway through the list, so a third mistake was not captured.
- For EME: explainer videos titled with buyer searches ("AI chat app for my community", "own your AI knowledge base"), one promo video on the site.

## Not relevant
The clip at 5:17 about national AI strategy and competition is general commentary, not sales guidance. Ignore it for EME marketing.

## Checklist when planning an EME campaign
1. Intro line: value + hook + presence.
2. A free example to 20 targets.
3. A path from entry offer to onboarding call to the larger offer.
4. Discovery questions before any pitch.
5. Four-question price survey.
6. Explainer videos by search term, one promo video.
